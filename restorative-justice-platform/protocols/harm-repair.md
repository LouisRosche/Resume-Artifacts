# Harm and Repair Circle Protocol

## Purpose
Address harm done, take accountability, support healing, and work toward repairing relationships and community.

## Duration
90-120 minutes

## Pre-Circle Requirements

### Individual Pre-Meetings Required With:
1. **Person who caused harm**:
   - Assess readiness to take accountability
   - Ensure understanding of impact
   - Prepare for listening to those affected

2. **Person(s) harmed**:
   - Assess readiness and desire to participate
   - Ensure emotional safety
   - Identify support people
   - Prepare for what they want to share

3. **Community members/support people**:
   - Explain their role
   - Prepare supportive questions

## Circle Structure

### 1. Opening & Setting Intention (15 minutes)
- Welcome and introductions
- Explain circle purpose
- Review guidelines and values:
  - Honesty and accountability
  - Respect and compassion
  - Focus on repair and healing
  - Confidentiality
- Establish safety agreements

### 2. Understanding the Harm (25-35 minutes)

**Round 1: What Happened (Factual)**
- Person who caused harm shares what they did
- No justifications or excuses
- Facilitator ensures clarity

**Round 2: Impact of Harm**
- Person(s) harmed share impact
- Others affected share their experience
- Person who caused harm listens without responding

**Round 3: Understanding Ripple Effects**
- "How has this affected the community?"
- "What has changed because of this harm?"

### 3. Taking Accountability (20-25 minutes)

**Questions for person who caused harm:**
- "What do you understand now about the impact of your actions?"
- "What led you to make those choices?"
- "What do you wish you had done differently?"
- "What do you want those you've harmed to know?"

**Open dialogue round:**
- Questions from those harmed
- Clarifications needed
- Expression of feelings

### 4. Repair and Moving Forward (25-30 minutes)

**Round 1: What's Needed**
- Person(s) harmed: "What do you need to heal?"
- Community: "What would help repair community trust?"

**Round 2: Accountability Actions**
- Person who caused harm: "What am I willing to do?"
- Specific, measurable commitments
- Timeline for actions

**Round 3: Support Plan**
- "What support does each person need?"
- Identify check-in schedule
- Clarify roles of support people

### 5. Agreement & Commitment (10-15 minutes)
- Written agreement of repair actions
- All parties sign agreement
- Set follow-up dates
- Define measures of success

### 6. Closing (5-10 minutes)
- Final round: "What are you taking from this circle?"
- Gratitude for participation and courage
- Reminder of next steps
- Closing ritual

## Critical Facilitator Skills

### Creating Safety
- Pre-assess all participants
- Be prepared to pause or stop if needed
- Have support plan for emotional distress
- Know limits of circle process

### Managing Power Dynamics
- Ensure person harmed has equal voice
- Don't allow justifications or minimizing
- Balance accountability with humanity
- Address any attempts to manipulate process

### Guiding Toward Repair
- Keep focus on future, not just past
- Push for specific, actionable commitments
- Ensure agreements are realistic
- Don't force forgiveness or reconciliation

## Follow-Up Protocol

### Week 1
- Individual check-ins with all participants
- Assess emotional state
- Begin accountability actions

### Week 3
- Progress check on commitments
- Address any barriers
- Adjust plan if needed

### Week 6
- Follow-up circle
- Review progress
- Celebrate successes
- Address remaining needs

### Month 3
- Final evaluation
- Close process or extend support

## When NOT to Use This Circle
- Immediate safety concerns present
- Criminal investigation ongoing
- Person who harmed shows no accountability
- Person harmed not ready/willing
- Significant power imbalance that can't be addressed
- Legal restrictions prevent process

## Alternative Approaches
- Individual restorative conversations
- Shuttle mediation
- Delayed circle after more preparation
- Referral to other supports (therapy, legal, etc.)
